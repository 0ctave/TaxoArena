  REGISTERED BAR: adopt sub-anchor reporting only if r exceeds the null's 95th pct AND exceeds 0.50
    exceeds null 95th pct: YES (+0.9147 vs +0.8764)
    exceeds 0.50:           YES (+0.9147)
  -> AS REGISTERED: PASSES both conditions

  BUT THE REGISTERED BAR IS UNINFORMATIVE AND I AM SAYING SO RATHER THAN BANKING THE PASS.
  The null sits at +0.8643. A RANDOM partition of the same shape reproduces almost the whole
  correlation, so the 0.50 bar was miscalibrated when I registered it: anything at all would clear
  it. The cause is a shrinkage artefact - a small cell pulls the whole BT vector toward zero, so
  the centred profile is roughly (s_c - 1) x theta_global with a cell-specific scalar, and both
  trees' profiles end up proportional to the SAME global ordering whatever the partition is. The
  margin over the null, +0.0504, is the only part of the primary that carries information.

=== (4) ADDED before its result was computed: is the reproducible structure CAPABILITY or JUDGE? ===
  The leading alternative explanation for (1b) is that both trees capture the same JUDGE artefact
  rather than the same capability. The x12 verdicts carry a measured verbosity preference and a
  measured position effect; if either clusters by topic, both trees would pick it up and the two
  profiles would agree without any capability structure underneath. That would also explain why
  neither leaf router beats the global best in (2).
  THE DISCRIMINATOR, and it is free: run the identical model-demeaned tree-robustness statistic on
  per-cell ANSWER-KEY ACCURACY instead of judge-derived BT strength. The key cannot have a judge
  artefact. If the key-derived profile reproduces at a similar level, the structure is capability.
  If it sits near the null while the judge-derived one does not, the profile is a judge artefact.

  key-derived model-demeaned r = +0.8143 over 24864 pairs
  its size-matched null over 200 reps: median +0.0183 | 95th pct +0.0352
  judge-derived was +0.4754 against a +0.0258 null.
  KEY  model-demeaned          observed +0.8143 | WITHIN-anchor null median +0.8130, 95th +0.8321
  KEY  (model, anchor)-demeaned observed +0.4135 | within-anchor null median +0.0146, 95th +0.0360
  -> CORRECTION: the key's +0.8143 is AT its within-anchor null. 'The structure is real and
     tree-robust' measured the ANCHOR profile, which the two trees share on 2,800 of 2,811
     questions. It said nothing about sub-anchor structure. The (model, anchor)-demeaned
     line is the only test of the registered question.
  -> THE KEY REPRODUCES TOO. The per-cell structure is a property of the QUESTIONS, not of the
     judge: the answer key, which cannot carry a judge artefact, agrees across trees at
     +0.8143 against a +0.0352 null. The judge-artefact explanation is rejected.
  RATIO judge/key = 0.58. A ratio far above 1 means the judge sees more 'structure' than the key
  supports, which is the signature of an artefact riding on top of real structure.

=== (5) ADDED before its result was computed: is the judge's shortfall FIDELITY or NOISE? ===
  (4) shows the judge recovers a fraction of a structure the key reproduces at +0.8143. Two very
  different readings: the judge TRANSMITS LESS of the real structure (a fidelity loss, which is
  a property of the judge and is improvable), or the judge's per-cell estimate is simply NOISIER
  because it rests on about three matches per question where the key rests on a fact. Those
  demand different responses, so separate them by measuring each instrument's own reliability.
  METHOD: under ONE tree, split each cell's questions into two halves, build the profile on each
  half, correlate them model-demeaned, and Spearman-Brown to full length. Then disattenuate the
  cross-tree correlation by that reliability. Identical treatment for both instruments.
  judge  split-half r=+0.0860 -> Spearman-Brown reliability R=+0.1583 | cross-tree +0.4754 -> disattenuated +3.0022
  key    split-half r=+0.7201 -> Spearman-Brown reliability R=+0.8373 | cross-tree +0.8143 -> disattenuated +0.9726

  THE DISATTENUATION IS INVALID AND ITS VERDICT IS DISCARDED. It returns +3.0022 for the judge,
  and a correlation cannot exceed 1. The assumption it breaks is independence: the two
  cross-tree profiles are built from the SAME questions and the SAME verdicts under two
  partitions that agree on 99.6% of anchor assignments, so they share almost all their data,
  while the split-half compares disjoint question sets. The two quantities are not on the
  same footing and their ratio means nothing. I am reporting this rather than the verdict it
  produced, which would have read 'NOISE, not fidelity'.

  WHAT (5) DOES ESTABLISH, and it is more important than what it was built to test:
    judge per-cell profile split-half reliability  r=+0.0860  (Spearman-Brown R=+0.1583)
    key   per-cell profile split-half reliability  r=+0.7201  (Spearman-Brown R=+0.8373)
  Measured on two INDEPENDENT halves of the same cell in the same tree, the judge's per-cell
  capability profile reproduces at +0.0860. At the x12 budget it is close to pure noise. The key's
  reproduces at +0.7201 on exactly the same split, so this is a property of the judge-derived
  estimate and not of the cells.
  AND THIS CUTS AGAINST (1b). The cross-tree judge correlation was +0.4754 - 5.5x its own
  independent-sample reliability of +0.0860. Two measurements that share almost all their data
  agree far better than two measurements that share none, which is what shared data does and
  not what reproducible structure does. (1b)'s reading that the judge profile 'carries real,
  tree-reproducible structure' is therefore NOT supported by (1b) alone.

=== (6) ADDED before its result was computed: restrict to questions the two trees DISAGREE about ===
  (5) shows the cross-tree comparison is contaminated by shared data. The clean version is to keep
  only the questions whose CELL ACTUALLY CHANGES between trees, where the two profiles are genuinely
  being asked to agree about something. If the agreement in (1b) came from shared cells it collapses
  here; if the profile is really tree-robust it survives.
  cell ids are not comparable across trees, so 'changed' is measured by the question's COMPANY:
  the Jaccard overlap between the set of questions sharing its cell in A and in B.
  most changed third (lowest overlap)    n=  690 | overlap 0.146 | model-demeaned judge +0.1895 key +0.6836 | (model,anchor)-demeaned judge -0.0841 key -0.0711
  middle third                           n=  691 | overlap 0.280 | model-demeaned judge +0.4815 key +0.8112 | (model,anchor)-demeaned judge +0.1554 key +0.5148
  least changed third (highest overlap)  n=  691 | overlap 0.585 | model-demeaned judge +0.7497 key +0.9406 | (model,anchor)-demeaned judge +0.7409 key +0.8513
  READ: if r falls as overlap falls, the cross-tree agreement is shared data. If r holds, the
  profile is genuinely tree-robust and (1b) stands. The KEY column is the decisive one: it says
  whether the structure itself is partition-dependent, or only the judge's estimate of it.

=== (7) BUDGET IMPLICATION: what would it take to resolve the leaf profile? ===
  (base: 8 leaves carried the split-half measurement, mean 71 questions each)
  to reach reliability 0.70: 12.4x the current per-cell evidence, i.e. ~883 questions per leaf
  to reach reliability 0.80: 21.3x the current per-cell evidence, i.e. ~1514 questions per leaf
  to reach reliability 0.90: 47.8x the current per-cell evidence, i.e. ~3408 questions per leaf
  (Spearman-Brown from the measured R=+0.1583, over 53 profiled leaves averaging 53 questions each)
  AND THE CAVEAT THAT MATTERS: this is a QUESTIONS-PER-CELL requirement, not only a call budget.
  Re-judging the same match does not add independent evidence - TEMP-0 showed the 5.7% test-retest
  floor is intrinsic and survives temperature 0 - so the multiplier has to come from new
  questions in each cell. The reserved pool holds 2811 questions across 53 profiled leaves; the
  0.80 target needs roughly 59764, which MMLU-Pro does not contain. A reliable per-leaf capability
  profile is therefore out of reach on this corpus at any call budget, which is a DATA limit and
  is the honest reason every leaf-level test in this programme has been underpowered.

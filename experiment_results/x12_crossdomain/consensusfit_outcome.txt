(4) what the consensus coefficient accounts for at the top of the board
      grok-4-1-fast-reasoning leads gemini-3.1-pro_5-shots by +0.301 theta while scoring 0.849 against 0.913 on the key.
      its consensus advantage is 0.882 - 0.863 = +0.018 agreement, worth +0.027 theta at the fitted coefficient,
      i.e. 9% of the gap. Equalising agreement alone does not reverse the order.

(5) DESCRIPTIVE, not registered - what the arm A intercept is standing in for
      On the 120 arm A matches where NEITHER the key nor the reference favours a side, the new contestant
      still wins 0.683 (the fitted intercept, which is not saturated, puts it at 0.803). Whatever that is, it is
      not consensus alignment: it is what makes the judge prefer that model with no evidence either way.
      THREE CANDIDATES, and this fit cannot separate them:
        - genuine quality the key does not capture;
        - style: its traces come from a different generation run than the twelve contestants;
        - POSITION: arm A was single order and the new contestant was ALWAYS Model A, so nothing cancelled
          the position effect. FLIP-PROV measured a 13.9% FLIP RATE (later decomposed by POSITION-DECOMP into
          ~6pp position and ~6pp noise) and ORDER-1 showed the board
          itself depends on which single order is kept (rho 0.958 against 0.986).
      Separating them costs one reversed-order pass over a sample of arm A. Until that is bought, the
      ARENA-13 (1) failure should be attributed to the single-order design as readily as to the reference.

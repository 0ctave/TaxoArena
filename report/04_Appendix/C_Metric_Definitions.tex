\chapter{Formal Metric Definitions}\label{app:metrics}

This appendix gives closed-form definitions for the statistics the settled chapters
quote and for the two construction exports still cited from them. Every arena number
these definitions govern comes from the settled eight-domain, eight-model
\texttt{RESOLVED} batch (Section~\ref{sec:res-runs}); each offline analysis names its
own inputs where it is reported. Bradley--Terry estimation itself --- the MM fit, the
Jeffreys-prior regularisation, and the variance correction --- is specified in
Section~\ref{sec:meth-bt} and Appendix~\ref{app:numerics} and is not repeated here.

\section{Key-Decidability, Agreement, and Tie Rate}\label{sec:app-metrics-decidable}

Two granularities of decidability share the name and are used strictly apart. A single
\emph{comparison} is key-decidable when exactly one of its two responses matches the
MMLU-Pro key; when both are right or both are wrong, the key abstains. A \emph{model
pair} is key-decidable in a domain at $N$ routed questions when the two accuracies
separate by more than twice their pooled binomial standard error,
$|p_1 - p_2| > 2\sqrt{p_1(1-p_1)/N + p_2(1-p_2)/N}$; the 200-of-224 decidable pairs of
Section~\ref{sec:res-correctness} use this pair-level test.

\emph{Agreement} is the fraction of key-decidable comparisons, among those on which
the judge committed to a winner, where that winner is the model that answered
correctly; the unconditional variant counts abstentions as disagreements and is quoted
only where named as such. The \emph{tie rate} is the fraction of logical comparisons
the judge rules a tie, reported stratified by comparison-level decidability
(Section~\ref{sec:res-tie-signature}). Both are proportions; their standard errors are
binomial, $\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}$, and are labelled as such wherever printed
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-metrics}).

\section{Rank Correlation and the Tie Conventions}\label{sec:app-metrics-rho}

Spearman $\rho$ between the fitted Bradley--Terry $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and
ground-truth accuracy $\mathbf{A}$ is the Pearson correlation of the two rank vectors,
with mid-ranks assigned to tied values; for tie-free rankings over $M$ models this
reduces to $\rho = 1 - 6\sum_i d_i^2 / (M(M^2-1))$ with $d_i$ the per-model rank
difference. The two \emph{tie conventions} of rule~D5
(Section~\ref{sec:meth-discipline}) govern how judge ties enter the Bradley--Terry
sufficient statistics before the fit: under \emph{half-weighting} each tie adds half a
win in both directions of its pair (the estimator's native accumulation); under
\emph{dropping}, tied comparisons are deleted before the counts are formed. Every
reported $\rho$ appears under both; on the settled batch the two coincide in every
domain and both arms (Section~\ref{sec:res-tie-policy}). At $M$ models $\rho$ moves on
a grid of spacing $6/(M(M^2-1))$ --- $0.0119$ at $M = 8$ --- an adjacent transposition
moves it by two grid steps, and differences below one step are not differences.

\section{Exact Binomial and Sign Tests}\label{sec:app-metrics-binomial}

Every paired-count test in this thesis is the same exact test. Among the $n$ paired
units on which the two sides disagree, $k$ favour the named side; under the null
$X \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, \tfrac{1}{2})$, and the two-sided $p$ is twice the
smaller tail probability at the observed $k$, capped at $1$. The 11-against-26 arm
split of Section~\ref{sec:res-c5} ($p = 0.020$) and the per-cell sign tests of
Section~\ref{sec:res-offsubject} ($14$/$13$, $p = 1.0$; $15$/$12$, $p = 0.70$) are
instances.

\section{Permutation Nulls and Empirical p-Values}\label{sec:app-metrics-permutation}

A permutation null draws $B$ random re-assignments matched to the observed
configuration and reports the one-sided empirical $p$: the fraction of draws at or
beyond the observed statistic in the registered direction, with zero qualifying draws
reported as an inequality rather than as zero, and Bonferroni correction within each
test family. Two analyses use this scheme: the leaf-substructure null ($B = 2{,}000$;
pseudo-leaves of exactly the observed sizes, pairwise question overlap matched to the
real leaves; Section~\ref{sec:res-leaf-null}) and the domain-reordering screen
($B = 1{,}500$; size-matched random subsets of the reserved pool, $p$ the fraction of
draws at or below the observed $\rho$; Section~\ref{sec:res-law}). The
rubric-specificity null instead compares the treatment arm against a size-matched
random-cell arm with a one-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ test
(Section~\ref{sec:res-rubric-null}).

\section{The Chance-Corrected Separation Score}\label{sec:app-metrics-J}

For a set $S$ of unit vectors with (possibly fractional) total count $n_S$, the
within-set scatter is $W(S) = n_S^2 - \lVert \sum_{x \in S} x \rVert^2$, the total
pairwise cosine dissimilarity. For a partition $\{S_c\}$ of $n$ vectors,
\[
J \;=\; 1 - \frac{\sum_c W(S_c)}{W(S)\,\sum_c n_c(n_c-1)\,/\,(n(n-1))},
\]
where the denominator is the exact expectation of the within-scatter under a uniformly
random partition into the same sizes; $J = 1$ for a perfect partition, $\approx 0$ for
a random one, exactly $0$ at $k = 1$. The whole-tree value ($J = 0.253129$ on the
frozen artifact, and the no-taxonomy-floor comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:res-construction}) evaluates this at $d = 256$ over the terminal
cells --- the 87 leaves plus any residual-holding internal nodes --- with construction
membership weights entering as fractional counts. The per-split acceptance gate uses
the same statistic over a node's proposed children; the gate, and the paired bootstrap
standard error of $\Delta J$ it thresholds on, are specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:dag-formalism}.

\section{Split-Half Reliability and Disattenuation}\label{sec:app-metrics-reliability}

The reliability of a cell-level ground-truth ranking at $n$ questions is modelled as
$r(n) = n/(n + c)$, estimated by splitting a cell's questions into random halves,
correlating the roster's two half-sample accuracy rankings, and lifting the
half-length correlation to full length by Spearman--Brown,
$r = 2r_{1/2}/(1 + r_{1/2})$; $c$ is then fitted across cell sizes. Disattenuation is
two-sided, $\rho_{\text{obs}}/r$, because both rankings being correlated are
finite-sample. The corrected constant is $c = 2.52$ at the eleven-model band; it is a
roster property and a ceiling rather than a prediction, with the caveats of
Appendix~\ref{app:corrections-register} and Section~\ref{sec:disc-debt}.

\section{Routing On-Subject Rate}\label{sec:app-metrics-onsubject}

A judged held-out question is \emph{on-subject} when its MMLU-Pro category equals the
category frozen at bootstrap on its cell's depth-1 ancestor --- the domain anchor the
cell sits under. The rate is the on-subject fraction of all judged questions:
$77.8\%$ on the settled batch (Section~\ref{sec:res-offsubject}).

\section{Total Dasgupta Cost}\label{sec:app-metrics-dasgupta}

The pipeline exports the whole-tree cost \parencite{dasgupta2016cost}
$\text{cost}(T) = \sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\,|T(v_{ij})|$ over unordered pairs of placed
queries, with $w_{ij}$ the cosine similarity clipped to $[0,1]$, $v_{ij}$ the deepest
common ancestor of the two queries' assigned nodes (a multi-member query is attributed
to its deepest containing node), and $|T(v)|$ the number of placed queries in the
subtree of $v$; lower is better. Above $5{,}000$ pairs the export is an unbiased
estimate: the mean contribution of $5{,}000$ uniformly sampled pairs rescaled by the
exact pair count. It is distinct from the acceptance score above, which is not a
Dasgupta quantity.

\section{Average Match Count}\label{sec:app-metrics-avgmatch}

Writing $\mathcal{L}(q)$ for the set of leaves holding query $q$,
$\text{AvgMatch} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} |\mathcal{L}(q_i)|$ over the $N$ unique
leaf-assigned construction queries; $1.0$ means every query resolves to a single leaf.
The frozen tree carries $9{,}141$ memberships over $8{,}299$ queries, so
$\text{AvgMatch} = 1.101$ (Appendix~\ref{app:frozen-tree}). The quantity governs the
construction corpus only: the arena withdraws secondary memberships before scoring, so
a routed held-out query contributes to exactly one cell.

\section{Routing Expected Calibration Error}\label{sec:app-metrics-ece}

Routing ECE tests whether the soft-routing distribution's confidence is calibrated
against its accuracy, at \emph{domain} granularity. For each query, its normalised
leaf membership weights are summed onto each leaf's depth-1 domain ancestors, yielding
a per-query distribution over domain categories in the same label space as the
MMLU-Pro category strings; the query's \emph{confidence} is the maximum domain mass,
and its prediction is correct when that argmax matches the query's category. Queries
are grouped into $B$ equal-width confidence bins, and
\[
\text{ECE} = \sum_{b=1}^{B} \frac{|\mathcal{B}_b|}{N}
\left| \text{acc}(\mathcal{B}_b) - \text{conf}(\mathcal{B}_b) \right|.
\]
No temperature-scaling recalibration is applied anywhere in this system.

\textbf{The exported number is not this metric.} The definition sums a query's leaf
membership onto each leaf's depth-1 domain ancestor, which is what makes the result a
distribution over domains. The export path instead takes the \emph{maximum} over a
domain's leaves, and a maximum over leaves is not a distribution: it does not
normalise, and it systematically understates confidence for queries spread across
several leaves of the same domain. The frozen run's exported $0.2114$ is therefore a
diagnostic with the discrepancy named, and no claim in this thesis rests on it
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-debt}). The direction of the error is known and its size is
not: fixing the aggregation raises every confidence, so apparent under-confidence
falls, by an amount nobody has measured. This is the full statement of the defect; the
other places it is mentioned point here.

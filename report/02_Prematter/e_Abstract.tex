Agentic systems dispatch sub-tasks to models and need a capability map
at a finer resolution than a scalar leaderboard provides. TaxoArena
induces a semantic partition over MMLU-Pro and runs an
LLM-as-a-judge arena inside its cells, so that every pairwise
comparison is judged under a rubric written for that cell. The
partition is the instrument; the result is a measurement of the judge.

On a corpus with a verifiable answer key, the judge's verdict tracks
answer correctness rather than reasoning quality. It agrees with the
key on $88.8\%$ of items where exactly one response is correct; its tie
rate roughly triples on the items where the key stops discriminating;
and its agreement is \emph{highest} where neither response contains any
reasoning at all --- $97.0\%$ against $83.2\%$ once model capability is
controlled. A judge given no cell rubric reaches the same winner on
$99.4\%$ of comparisons, and a paired arm that removes the partition
entirely matches the key to within half a percentage point
($94.2\%$ against $94.7\%$) on an identical question set. Under the
registered decision rule the rank-correlation contrast on Math formally
failed the null in favour of the partition-free arm; the gap collapses
to the registered boundary once ties are dropped, an attribution made
after the fact and marked as such.

The construction reaches a certified structural fixed point and yields
87 cells whose induced rubrics are cell-specific against a size-matched
randomised null ($p = 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$). What fails --- under
pre-registration, and before the arena ran --- is the premise that
cells carry different true model rankings: between-cell rank agreement
is flat across an elevenfold change in granularity. On a
multiple-choice corpus a capable judge can shortcut to correctness and
bypass the partition entirely; the architecture binds only where
correctness is not checkable. Results are a selected-domain study at
one seed.
